## One aromatic core

Oil of wintergreen (**methyl salicylate**), salicylic acid, and aspirin all share the same benzene-ring core carrying a carbonyl group — the reactions in this experiment only change the *periphery* of that core. That makes aspirin a friendly first look at how chemists build a useful molecule from a natural starting material.

**Step 1 (hydrolysis):** oil of wintergreen is boiled with $\ce{NaOH}$ and then acidified, cleaving its ester to give **salicylic acid**. **Step 2 (condensation / esterification):** salicylic acid is heated with **acetic anhydride** and a drop of acid catalyst, which acetylates its phenol –OH to give **aspirin** plus acetic acid. Aspirin itself (first marketed by Bayer in 1899) is one of the most widely used medicines in the world.

## 2 · Mechanism &amp; pathway

The aspirin-forming step is a **nucleophilic acyl substitution** (an esterification): the phenol oxygen of salicylic acid attacks a carbonyl carbon of acetic anhydride, and acetate leaves.

<div data-mount="pathway"></div>

<div class="mech-grid">
<article class="mech-step"><span class="step-num">1</span>
<h3>Activate the carbonyl</h3>
<p>A trace of acid catalyst (conc. $\ce{H3PO4}$) protonates a carbonyl of <strong>acetic anhydride</strong>, making its carbon more electrophilic — easier for a weak nucleophile like a phenol to attack.</p>
</article>
<article class="mech-step major-accent"><span class="step-num">2</span>
<h3>Phenol attacks → ester</h3>
<p>The <strong>phenol –OH</strong> of salicylic acid attacks that carbonyl carbon. The anhydride breaks, transferring a <strong>–COCH₃ (acetyl)</strong> group to the oxygen and forming the new <strong>ester</strong> bond of aspirin.</p>
</article>
<article class="mech-step minor-accent"><span class="step-num">3</span>
<h3>Acetic acid leaves</h3>
<p>The other half of the anhydride departs as <strong>acetic acid</strong>, and the catalyst is regenerated. Net: salicylic acid + acetic anhydride → aspirin + acetic acid.</p>
</article>
</div>

<div class="callout info">
<h3>Why acetic anhydride instead of acetic acid?</h3>
<p>You could esterify with acetic acid directly, but that equilibrium is slow and unfavorable. <strong>Acetic anhydride</strong> is a much better acetylating agent: its leaving group (acetate/acetic acid) makes the acylation fast and essentially irreversible, so the reaction finishes in minutes. Excess anhydride is destroyed with water at the end (it hydrolyzes to harmless acetic acid).</p>
</div>

<div class="callout">
<h3>Reading it by FTIR (how you prove it worked)</h3>
<p>Salicylic acid and oil of wintergreen show a broad hydrogen-bonded <strong>O–H band (≈3000–3500 cm⁻¹)</strong> and a single C=O near <strong>1700 cm⁻¹</strong>. <strong>Aspirin loses the broad O–H</strong> (the phenol is now an ester) and shows <strong>two distinct C=O peaks</strong> — one ester, one carboxylic acid. That change is the fingerprint that the acetylation happened.</p>
</div>

## 3 · Procedure

The two-step general-chemistry procedure from the Olmsted (1998) experiment. Always use your instructor's version and your lab's safety rules — this is a study aid, not a substitute for a supervised SOP.

<a class="dl-btn" href="docs/synthesis-of-aspirin-lab-manual.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">⬇ Download the full lab handout (PDF)</a>

| Reagent | Amount | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Salicylic acid | 1.4 g (10.1 mmol) | substrate (the phenol/acid) |
| Acetic anhydride | 3.0 mL (≈3.2 g, 31.7 mmol, ~3 equiv) | acetylating agent (excess) |
| Conc. phosphoric acid, $\ce{H3PO4}$ | 5 drops | acid catalyst |
| Deionized water | as needed | quench &amp; recrystallize |

### Step 1 — Oil of wintergreen → salicylic acid (hydrolysis)

1. Weigh ~**4 mL oil of wintergreen** into a clean, dry 250-mL beaker.
2. Add **40 mL of 6 M $\ce{NaOH}$** with stirring (a precipitate forms); heat to a **gentle boil for 15 min**, reducing the rate to avoid bumping.
3. Cool in an ice bath, then slowly add **50 mL of 8 M $\ce{H2SO4}$** with stirring (salicylic acid precipitates).
4. Chill, then isolate the solid by **suction filtration (Büchner funnel)**; rinse with iced water.
5. **Recrystallize:** dissolve the crude in ~100 mL hot water, cool, chill, isolate, and dry overnight.

### Step 2 — Salicylic acid → aspirin (acetylation)

1. Weigh **1.4 g salicylic acid** into a dry 125-mL Erlenmeyer flask.
2. In the **fume hood**, add **3.0 mL acetic anhydride** (caustic vapors) and **5 drops conc. $\ce{H3PO4}$**.
3. Stopper with tubing; float the flask in a water bath and hold at **85–90 °C for 5 min** (do not boil).
4. Stop heating and add **2 mL water** through the tubing to destroy excess acetic anhydride.
5. Cool, add **20 mL water**; let crystals form, then add **10 mL water**, swirl, and chill.
6. Isolate the crude aspirin by suction filtration; rinse with iced water.
7. **Recrystallize** in **~10 mL water per gram**, then **oven-dry at 80 °C for 1 h** (aspirin melts/decomposes at ~100 °C); cool and weigh.

**Characterize:** record mass and percent yield (calculator below); take an FTIR spectrum (loss of broad O–H, two C=O peaks); optionally a melting point.

<div class="safety"><strong>Safety:</strong> acetic anhydride gives caustic vapors (fume hood); conc. $\ce{H3PO4}$ and $\ce{H2SO4}$ are corrosive, 6 M $\ce{NaOH}$ is caustic; keep the aspirin oven below 100 °C. Goggles and gloves. <strong>Waste:</strong> the acidic Step-1 filtrate to the designated acidic-aqueous waste; other benign liquid waste per your lab's rules.</div>
